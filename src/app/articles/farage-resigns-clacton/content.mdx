# The Member for Clacton, for now

**Nigel Farage resigned his seat to fight it again at a by-election. Here is what a resignation actually does to the parliamentary record — and how to watch it happen in the data.**

*Henry Hudson · Henceforth · July 2026*

Nigel Farage announced on 7 July that he is resigning as the MP for Clacton and will stand in the by-election that follows. The move is his answer to press reports about undeclared support during the year before his 2024 election, now the subject of an investigation by Parliament's standards watchdog; he denies any wrongdoing, saying he has "done nothing wrong" and has "not broken the law in any way at all." The politics of it will be everywhere this week. This article is about the plumbing: what a resignation actually is, what it does to the official record, and how [The Hansard](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/the-hansard/id6762037651) will carry it.

## You cannot actually resign

By long-standing rule of the House, a Member cannot simply hand in a letter. Instead they apply for appointment as Crown Steward and Bailiff of the Chiltern Hundreds (or of the Manor of Northstead) — a nominal "office of profit under the Crown" whose acceptance disqualifies its holder from sitting in the Commons. The seat is vacant from the moment the Chancellor of the Exchequer grants the warrant, not from the moment of the announcement. A resignation, in the data, is an appointment.

## What the record says

For a day after the announcement, the official Members data service still listed Farage as the **Current Member** for Clacton, with no end date on his membership. That was not a lag bug and not a denial; it was the mechanism above working as designed. The announcement was a video; the record changes when the warrant is signed. It was signed the next day. The record now closes his membership on **8 July 2026**, and the end reason it gives, verbatim, is *"Resignation (Northstead)"* — the office he took turned out to be the Manor of Northstead, not the Chiltern Hundreds; either disqualifies. Clacton is vacant.

The Hansard's bundled roster, refreshed for this week's update against the same official source on 7 July — the day of the announcement, a day before the warrant — still lists 650 sitting Members, Clacton among them. That is what a snapshot is; how the vacancy reaches the app is below.

The numbers the record does hold: Farage took Clacton on 4 July 2024 with 21,225 votes (46.2%) to the Conservatives' 12,820 and Labour's 7,448 — a majority of 8,405 on a turnout of 45,958 from a 78,245-strong electorate. A Reform UK gain, and the constituency's current baseline. Whatever happens at the by-election happens against those numbers.

## What happens to Clacton

With the warrant granted, Clacton has no MP until the by-election returns one — constituents keep their council, their casework lands with the House authorities, and the seat sits empty on the map. The writ for the by-election is moved in the Commons, and polling typically follows within weeks. Farage has said he will be on the ballot. The seat's history does not empty with it: his votes, written questions, and debate contributions stay in the record permanently — a parliamentary career is append-only, even when it pauses.
