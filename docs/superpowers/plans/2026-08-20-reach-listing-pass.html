The reach lever — listing pass for Henceforth and The Hansard

Two live apps with five-star ratings and zero recorded downloads through 18 August. The store listing is the only lever not yet pulled. This pass reads what the listings say today, applies the one field editable on a live version immediately, and stages the rest word for word for next Wednesday's version records.

How the fields rank for search. Apple weighs the app name heaviest, the subtitle second, the keyword field third; the description is not indexed for search but sells the tap. Promotional text (170 characters) is the only field editable on a live version without review — it changed today. Name, subtitle and keywords change only with a version submission, so those wait for 4.53 and 1.9 on Wednesday 26 August.

Henceforth

  • Subtitle: Programmable Bitcoin wallet (27 characters). The current "A mobile forth" spends the second-heaviest search field on a word almost nobody searches; "programmable", "Bitcoin" and "wallet" are the app's three searchable truths, and the Forth identity keeps the name field plus the keyword.

Gains the high-volume terms crypto, btc, blockchain, terminal, calculator, learn; drops the space-wasting comma-space format and the words the subtitle now carries.

The Hansard

  • Subtitle: keep See what your MP is doing — action-led, plain, and carries the highest-volume term the app can honestly claim. No change staged.

Gains westminster, news, mps, debates; drops the low-search jargon (division, petition, constituency, democracy) while keeping postcode, which is how people actually search for finding their member.

Draft: "Every Wednesday the front page carries This Week in Parliament, a digest of the week's divisions, questions and answers, written from the official record."

How to know it worked

The reach numbers in the morning report are the assertion: today's baseline is zero recorded downloads for both apps through 18 August. Two to three weeks after the Wednesday submission carries the staged fields, the same daily pull answers whether the lever moved anything. If it did not, the next levers in order of cost: localised keyword fields (the single en-GB locale leaves every other storefront unindexed), an App Store search ads test with a tiny budget, and the screenshots' first two frames (the only ones most users ever see).
