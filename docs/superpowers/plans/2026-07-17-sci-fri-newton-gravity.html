The science, checked before the pixels

The film’s quantitative spine is Newton’s own Moon test, and it must be numerically true on screen:

  • An apple falls 4.9 metres in its first second (½ × 9.8 m/s²).
  • The Moon sits about 60 Earth radii away. If gravity weakens with the square of distance, the Moon should fall 1/3600th as fast: 4.9 m ÷ 3600 ≈ 1.36 millimetres in one second.
  • The Moon’s actual deviation from a straight line, from its orbital period (27.3 days) and radius: ≈ 1.4 millimetres per second. The inverse square is not assumed — it is measured against the sky.
  • From inverse-square + circular motion, Kepler’s third law falls out in three lines: force needed = force supplied → the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the distance. The film shows the third-law line from last week’s own figure being re-drawn by Newton’s law.
