The road to Maxwell

Henry's steer: electricity, Maxwell eventually, but something simpler first. The road: close Newton's arc with its two conservations, then walk into electricity the way history did, one simple discovery per 97-second film, so Maxwell arrives as the season finale and the reveal that the whole season was walking toward light.

The standing rules apply to every film. One idea per episode, story first; words before symbols, symbols as finales; no voiceover, text and music only; a NEW public-domain recording each week, period-matched to the discovery; the gardening rule keeps the next cut staged before the current one posts. Cuts stage on the Desktop; the pipeline is FeynmanFigures.
The locked format (2026-07-24) anchors every film above the one idea: the PERSON woven in with a true story beat; the law in the discoverer's own published words, morphing to symbols; a test or true-number demonstration the viewer watches; and real period paintings, licence-verified, lots of them, slow push-ins with artist and collection credits. The electricity chain is rich in all four: Coulomb's torsion balance, Galvani's frog plates, Oersted's lecture bench, Faraday's Royal Institution, and a century of portraiture. One recycled asset: the shelved vector-potential cut on the Desktop is earmarked for rework into this format in the Faraday and Maxwell weeks.

The season: films 4 to 10

Film 4 · Friday 4 September · cut in the week of 31 August

What the third law was hiding

One idea: conservation of momentum. Equal and opposite forces mean the universe's total momentum never changes; the third law was a conservation law in disguise. The pictures: a cannon recoiling, two skaters pushing apart and drifting away in exact proportion to their masses, a rocket that works in empty space because it throws its own fuel backwards. The reveal: nothing in the universe can change its momentum without handing the difference to something else. Period music: Newtonian era baroque; a Purcell or Handel recording (public-domain performance, verified at cut).

Film 5 · Friday 11 September · cut in the week of 7 September

The number that never changes

One idea: conservation of energy. A pendulum trades height for speed and back, and the sum never cheats; energy is not a thing but a number, and the number is always the same. Feynman called it the strangest law in physics. The pictures: the pendulum with its energy bar constant while its two halves seesaw; a rollercoaster; the same accounting done three ways. The reveal: physics can be done by bookkeeping alone. Period music: the law crystallised in the 1840s (Mayer, Joule, Helmholtz); a Schumann or Mendelssohn recording.

Film 6 · Friday 18 September · cut in the week of 14 September

The other inverse square law

One idea: Coulomb's law, taught as gravity's twin. The audience has spent five weeks with one inverse square law; here is the same equation in a different coat, charge in place of mass, and vastly stronger, with the one new wrinkle that charge comes in two kinds and can repel. The pictures: the two formulas side by side morphing into one another; rubbed amber lifting scraps of paper; the word electricity coming from elektron, Greek for amber. The reveal: nature reused her favourite equation. Period music: Coulomb measured it in the 1780s; a Mozart or Haydn recording.

Film 7 · Friday 25 September · cut in the week of 21 September

The frog and the battery

One idea: current — electricity made to flow. Galvani's frog legs twitch on a copper hook; he thinks the electricity is in the animal; Volta thinks it is in the metals, and proves it by stacking coins and brine-soaked cloth into the first battery. A wrong idea and a right one, arguing each other into the modern world. The reveal: the argument itself was the discovery; every battery since is Volta's pile. Period music: 1790s to 1800; early Beethoven.

Film 8 · Friday 2 October · cut in the week of 28 September

The compass that flinched

One idea: electricity moves magnets. Oersted, mid-lecture in 1820, notices a compass needle twitch when he closes a circuit; the two great mysteries of the age turn out to be one subject. The pictures: the needle swinging as the switch closes, the circular field wrapping the wire. The reveal: the effect is sideways — nature's first hint that fields curl. Period music: 1820; Beethoven or Schubert.

Film 9 · Friday 9 October · cut in the week of 5 October

Running it backwards

One idea: induction. If electricity moves magnets, Faraday asks whether magnets can make electricity, and after ten years of trying finds the answer: yes, but only while something CHANGES. A moving magnet lights a coil; a still one does nothing. The reveal: nearly every watt in your house is made exactly this way, a magnet spinning near a coil. Period music: 1831; Chopin.

Film 10 · Friday 16 October · the season finale, cut in the week of 12 October

And then there was light

One idea: Maxwell's synthesis. The season's discoveries condense, one by one, into four short equations — the symbols-as-finale rule at full stretch, every symbol having been WORDS in an earlier film. Maxwell solves them and a wave comes out; the wave's speed comes out of the electrical measurements alone; and it is the speed of light. The reveal: light is electromagnetism — the whole season was walking toward this sentence. Period music: 1865; Brahms.

The two artifacts the season leaves behind

The physics app. Every film is built in Swift in FeynmanFigures, and the parts are already app-shaped: pure, tested physics cores (KeplerOrbit, GasWorld), deterministic scene views per film, and the scrubber pattern that lets a finger drive any frame. By Maxwell the project holds interactive demonstrations of orbits, the three laws, momentum, energy, Coulomb's balance, Oersted's needle, Faraday's coil and the field equations. The end-of-season act is an assembly pass: a menu over the scenes, the film timelines given their interaction layers (the scrubber generalised), and "sort of a physics app" becomes an actual one. Whether it then joins the Wednesday cadence as a fourth App Store app is the season-end decision, alongside choosing the next season.

The Henceforth vocabulary. The formulas already live as words: ~/Programming/FORTH/feynman-equations.fs (twenty-plus words — momentum, kinetic-energy, gravity-force, torque and inertia — with the vendored copy pinned by FeynmanEquations_Tests on Henceforth main). The season's rule: each film's formula lands as a word with a test the same week it airs. Film six is the vocabulary's best moment: coulomb-force takes exactly the stack shape of gravity-force, so the film's one idea — nature reused her favourite equation — is visible in the source code.
