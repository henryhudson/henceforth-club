Implementation plan · 2026-07-14 · /text · site + app + process · from a four-lens research pass

Making /text smooth

The research verdict in one line: the funnel is inverted. Best-in-class archivers let the reward happen first and ask for money second; /text asks a stranger to understand Bitcoin SV before showing them their own posts — and its primary button currently dead-ends at a disabled stub. Five phases, ordered by leverage: stop the bleeding today, invert the web funnel, earn trust, smooth the app, harden the process.

For agentic workers: REQUIRED SUB-SKILL — use superpowers:subagent-driven-development or superpowers:executing-plans. Steps are real checkboxes. Repositories: henceforth-club (every push deploys production in ~30 seconds) and Henceforth (money app — full unit suite green before push, adversarial review on money-path diffs). No acronyms in prose. Errors as values in pure cores. Every task ends with the named test green.

Where this came from: a five-agent research pass on 2026-07-14 — a first-time-visitor walk of the live site against src/app/text/**, the app-side journey read from origin/main, the operational ledger (board cards, corrections ledgers, thirty text: commits since 2026-07-01), external patterns (archive.today, Save Page Now, ArDrive Turbo, client-side X-export viewers, crypto checkout guides), and a completeness critic. Forty findings, synthesised here into thirty tasks.

The shape of the problem

lensthe clunk, in one sentence
Site funnelThe “Archive yours” accent button leads to a stub; conversion sits below thirty witness posts; prices are satoshi-only (the one fiat figure is computed from a hardcoded GBP_PER_USD = 0.79); the drop zone cannot accept a drop, nor the zip X actually sends; a refresh mid-payment loses the paid job.
App flowThe fetch fee auto-pays with no consent moment; a paid archive dies on swipe-dismiss or restart; re-runs re-buy reads to learn nothing changed; the multi-batch wait is blind; the finish-line URL is not tappable; the terminal is a mandatory gate.
ProcessOne settlement protocol lives in two repos and has already drifted (forfeiting redeemable fees); /text served 500 for a day with no monitor; the budget is a manual Vercel edit; founding votes need manual replays; the verification gates themselves lie (hook, disk, simulators).
External evidenceThe winners all do the same four things: instant local preview before any payment, tangible fiat pricing, a permanent URL minted at payment time, and per-item receipts verifiable on an independent explorer.
CriticNobody had covered: archived profiles are invisible to search and unfurl bare; the web path has no handle-claim rules (permanent impersonation is one upload away); mobile is the real funnel and the design is desktop-first; the empty-wallet first-run is a wall; nothing brings an archiver back.

Phase 1 — invert the web funnel the flagship

The external evidence is unanimous: reward first, payment second. A visitor drops the zip X emailed them, sees their own archive rendered instantly and locally, gets a tangible quote, pays, and walks away with a permanent link that fills in live. Target: this is the /text a stranger meets two Wednesdays from now.

Task 8 · site medium

Accept the actual export zip, client-side

dropZone.ts · ArchiveFlow.tsx:93-97 (fflate is already a dependency)
  • Unzip in the browser; pull data/tweets.js, data/profile.js, data/account.js; keep the three-file path as fallback. Streaming unzip with a text-first fast path (tweets.js is small; defer media) so multi-gigabyte exports survive mobile Safari.
  • Say the privacy sentence plainly: “Nothing leaves your browser until you pay to inscribe.” Every smooth competitor leads with it.
Task 9 · site medium

Instant local preview before any mention of money

  • Drop → parsed archive rendered showroom-style from local state, before payment or upload. Payment is step two, introduced while the visitor looks at their own posts.
  • Itemised live quote derived from the parse: “1,204 posts + 87 photos → ~2.1 MB on-chain → £0.30 once, readable forever”, with layer toggles matching the app.
Task 10 · site medium

Addressable jobs: a refresh must never lose a payment

ArchiveFlow.tsx:43-60 · /api/text/job/[id] (already returns everything the view needs)
  • Push ?job=<id> (or /text/archive/<jobId>) once quoted; hydrate on mount from the job endpoint so reload, navigation, and shared links resume the live status — including after money was sent.
Task 11 · site medium

Checkout that matches how people actually pay

  • Large QR (URI with amount) + copy button on desktop; on touch devices the bsv: deep link is the button and the QR is secondary (nobody scans their own screen).
  • Three-state status that survives reload: awaiting payment → received, confirming → inscribing.
Task 12 · site medium

Mint the permanent URL at payment time; let it fill in live

  • The moment payment confirms, present henceforth.club/text/<handle> as a live shareable page showing “inscribing 214/1,204 posts” and filling as batches confirm. Save Page Now hands back the permanent URL before the save completes — the reward arrives at payment, and every early share is free distribution.

Phase 2 — trust and growth

Task 13 · site medium · GATES SPEC B

Handle-claim rules before the QR opens

xBinding.ts · xOwner.ts · the spec B go-live gate
  • An upload for a handle already bound to a key is rejected (or quarantined pending the owner’s signature).
  • Unbound uploads render with an explicit “unverified — claimed by upload, not by @handle” badge; account.js identity must match the claimed handle.
  • Precedence rules written into the spec. Permanence makes every impersonation permanent — this is a go-live item, not polish.
Task 14 · site small

Receipts: permanence shown, not claimed

  • Per-post “inscribed <date> · block N · verify” linking to WhatsOnChain; page-level downloadable receipt (handle, post count, sats, transaction ids).
  • One line of permanence copy: “These posts live on the Bitcoin SV blockchain. This page just reads them — anyone can.”
Task 15 · site small

Archived profiles become findable and shareable

sitemap.ts (no /text URLs today) · /text/[handle]/page.tsx (no openGraph; the Hansard ImageResponse pattern is in the repo)
  • Sitemap fed from the archive registry: every profile and post page indexed.
  • openGraph + twitter metadata and an opengraph-image.tsx for profiles (avatar, name, post count, “on Bitcoin — permanent”).
  • Share button (navigator.share on mobile, copy-link fallback).
Task 16 · site small + medium

Posts read like posts; archives market each other

  • Substitute expanded_url for t.co strings at parse time (improves what gets inscribed too) and linkify URLs in PostEntry.
  • Rewrite @mentions pointing at already-archived handles to their showroom pages; unarchived mentions get a quiet “not yet archived — archive them” affordance (the tweetback pattern; a growth loop in the product surface).

Phase 3 — the app flow 4.47 / 4.48

Task 17 · app medium · APP STORE GATE

The consent moment: no coin moves without a yes

xtextWord.swift:203, 641-680 · XArchiveSheet.swift
  • The free quote is the natural consent point: render it in the sheet (“Reading @handle costs ~N sats (≈ £x) — separate from the inscription price”) with an explicit confirm before paidArchiveFetch broadcasts. This is the standing 4.47 gate: the fee must not auto-pay for strangers.
Task 18 · app small, then large

A paid archive must survive the user

  • Now: interactiveDismissDisabled while pendingXArchive is armed + a “Discard this fetched archive? Re-fetching costs another fee” confirm.
  • Then: persist the fetched archive to disk keyed by handle with a validity window; re-runs re-hydrate instead of re-buying (media references are free CDN fetches — persisting refs alone already saves the fee).
Task 19 · app small

Re-runs stop buying reads to learn nothing changed

  • Short-circuit when the archived post count covers the quote’s readable posts: a free “all N posts on-chain — nothing new” sheet state, PAY disarmed. With task 17, a re-run that does have new posts becomes “archive the N new posts for ~X sats” — the delta offer that also anchors retention (task 24).
Task 20 · app large

The sheet owns the whole flow; the terminal becomes the power-user alias

  • Handle field at the top of XArchiveSheet, reachable from a wallet or showroom button; s" handle" xtext pre-fills it. Transcript-only moments (fee, batch txids, verify prompt, final URL) become sheet states; the transcript keeps the precise record.
  • Two-register messaging throughout: plain words + fiat in the sheet, precise jargon in the transcript.
Task 21 · app medium

The empty-wallet first run stops being a wall

  • Insufficient-funds state on the quote sheet: “you need ~N more sats (≈ £x)” + concrete acquisition guidance (named wallets/exchanges that sell Bitcoin SV to United Kingdom cards, or a receive QR to fund from an existing wallet). Plausibly the largest silent drop-off on the app funnel today; nobody measures it.

Phase 4 — harden the process

Task 22 · both repos medium

One settlement contract, consumed by both sides

  • A shared fixture (gate arm → burned-or-redeemable) that the Swift XArchiveWiring tests and the xGate.ts tests both consume, extending the existing cost-parity pattern — a reorder of gate arms then fails the app suite in the same push. The drift has already happened once and was one funded run from forfeiting a redeemable ~$8 fee.
Task 23 · ops medium

A scheduled low-funds staging e2e, so the funded run verifies economics, not correctness

  • Recurring scripted quote→pay→gate→read loop against the production gate with a tiny archive and a low-funds wallet (the no-private-keys rule already permits exactly this). The manual funded PAY has been a days-open singular human gate while both sides changed under it.
Task 24 · site + app medium

Retention: the archive that grows and reports back

  • Delta offer on both surfaces from the cache’s known last post (“34 new posts unarchived — top up for ~N sats”).
  • Opt-in notification when a bound archive earns votes. A one-shot purchase becomes a subscription-shaped habit without a subscription.
Task 25 · ops small each

The small process debts, named

  • Daily X budget becomes a Redis operator value (no Vercel redeploy to change it); the strategic posture goes on a card: the export path is the scalable route, the paid X-API fetch a capped convenience.
  • Founding votes minted inside /api/x/register (the handler already fetches the archive and extends the cache) — no more ledger replays.
  • WhatsOnChain contract tests pinning the five known traps (script truncation, absent prevouts, unix seconds, batched fees, >2 MB uncacheable); derived values persisted at write time, never re-derived per view.
  • Pre-push hook distinguishes package-resolution failure from test failure; mini workflow gains a disk-space preflight that fails fast with a named cause.
  • Spec B go-live gate split: automation stages the launchd plist, scripts the low-funds e2e, and ticks the plan checkboxes as tasks land; Henry’s list shrinks to the Keychain seed and the sign-off.
