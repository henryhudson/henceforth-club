This Week in Parliament — the one-page overview

Design · 2026-07-01 · a single-A4, plain-English overview becomes the standard weekly issue on henceforth.club; the existing long-form article is demoted to a linked reference where it exists · open decisions flagged inline

One-line: Each week's page at /hansard/this-week/[week] becomes a one-page overview: headline, a few lines on the top story, a brief of the week's statements and Prime Minister's Questions, the divisions as a graphic, a department bar chart, the most-asked subjects, the top questions with the minister's reply, a feature, and a two-line footer of the busiest asker and answerer. Plain English throughout, sized to fit one A4 and print from the browser. The rich long-form article is kept, moved to /hansard/this-week/[week]/full, and reached by a link at the top of the overview only for the weeks that have one.

Status: the layout and plain-English style are proven — a scratch renderer already produces all six existing weeks (recess, four-division, eight, nine and twelve-division), verified by screenshot. This spec covers folding that renderer into the Next.js app as the standard page, with the prose living in the digest and produced by the generator.

The shift

Today /hansard/this-week/[week] renders the full long-form digest through DigestView, and it returns a 404 for any issue whose status is not published. The change makes the one-page overview the thing a reader lands on. The long-form article is not deleted. It moves one click away, to a /full sub-route, and the overview links to it at the top for the weeks where it exists. New weeks are overview-only, so they carry no link.

Nothing is thrown away. The digest JSON stays the single source of truth. It gains one small optional block for the overview's plain-English prose. The existing rich fields stay exactly where they are and feed the /full page.

The page

One A4, in reading order:

  1. Masthead. The kicker "This Week in Parliament, {window}", the headline, and a stats line of divisions, written questions and distinct askers.
  2. Full-article link. "Read the full article" at the top, shown only where a /full page exists for the week.
  3. Top story. The plain-English overview.intro as a drop-cap lead paragraph.
  4. The week in brief. A short band of the week's oral set-pieces — government statements, urgent questions and Prime Minister's Questions — each a bold lead-in and a sentence or two, from overview.brief. This is where a moment like the "Did you meet Andy?" exchange at Prime Minister's Questions lands.
  5. Divisions and department chart, side by side. The divisions adapt to the count: six or fewer render as the large pill cards, seven or more collapse to compact one-line rows, and past sixteen the sixteen most-contested show with a summary line for the rest. Always sorted closest-margin first. The chart is a horizontal bar per department, its label column auto-sized so no name can clip.
  6. The most-asked subjects. A wrapped band of the top headings with their counts, derived from topTopics.
  7. Top questions. Two or three featured written questions, each with the minister's actual reply trimmed to complete sentences, from the digest's existing qa field. This is where the reader gets past the counts and into what an MP asked and what the government said back — the road-surfaces-softening question, the infected-blood one.
  8. The feature. From overview.feature, its summary trimmed to the first few sentences with a bracket-safe cut that never severs a parenthesis.
  9. Footer. Two plain lines — the member who tabled the most written questions and the minister who answered the most.

A recess week has no divisions, chart or feature. It shows the headline "Parliament in recess", the stats line of zeros, and the plain-English intro, and nothing else.

Data model

One optional block is added to DigestData in src/lib/this-week/types.ts. Everything else the overview needs — stats, departments, topTopics, highlights.votes — it already has.

overview?: {
  headline: string              // may differ from the long-form headline
  intro: string                 // plain English, the top story in a few lines
  brief?: { title: string; when: string; note: string }[]   // oral set-pieces: statements, urgent questions, PMQs
  feature?: { title: string; summary: string }
  mostActive?: {
    asker:    { name: string; party: string; count: number }
    answerer: { name: string; party: string; count: number }
  }
}

The overview renders from overview plus structured fields the digest already has — stats, departments, topTopics, highlights.votes, and qa for the top-questions section. New to the digest are the plain-English prose, the oral brief, and the two busiest names. Everything else is derived. If overview is absent on a published issue, the page falls back to the long-form intro and qa so the site never shows a blank page during migration.

Generation and migration

Going forward. The editorial pass in scripts/this-week/PROMPT.md gains one step: after writing the digest, it writes the overview block in plain English under the style rules below — the intro, the feature, and the oral brief of the week's statements, urgent questions and Prime Minister's Questions. The qa for the top-questions section is already produced as part of the digest, so nothing new is needed there. The wrapper, run-weekly.sh, computes mostActive deterministically from the questions-statements API — the busiest asker by questions tabled in the window, the busiest answerer by questions answered — the same tally already proven by hand, and injects it. One run produces the digest and its overview together, so the two cannot drift.

The six existing weeks. A one-time backfill script writes the overview block into each. The intro and feature come from the plain-English rewrites already produced. Each week's brief is composed from its long-form proceedings, and the 1 July week reuses the brief from the mock. Each week's two busiest names are computed. The long-form fields are left untouched, so every existing week keeps its /full page.

Print

Browser print, no server-side PDF. The sheet declares @page { size: A4 } and print styles. A4Sheet runs a small script on load that shrinks the base font in small steps until the content height fits one A4, so the page is guaranteed to print as a single sheet whatever the division count. A "Print" button calls window.print(). This is exactly the mechanism the scratch renderer uses and prints correctly today.

Space budget. The fuller content — brief, subjects, top questions and feature — makes the page denser. The auto-fit absorbs the variance down to a readable floor. If a heavy week would still overflow at that floor, a defined trim order keeps the one-page guarantee, applied in order until it fits: shorten the top-questions answers, then drop from three featured questions to two, then collapse the most-asked-subjects band into a single line. The intro, the brief, the divisions and the feature are never trimmed. The screenshot pass on the twelve-division week is the check that the budget holds.

Style rules for the overview prose

The overview.intro and overview.feature.summary follow the plain-English house rules, and the generator prompt states them:

  • Full sentences and plain English. Short sentences. Full stops freely.
  • No em-dashes or en-dashes as connectors. No colons to introduce or join clauses.
  • Commas used sparingly.
  • Whole numbers up to ninety-nine spelled out. Numerals kept for vote tallies, dates, percentages and large or precise figures.
  • Proper terms for role and party initialisms: Members of Parliament, Prime Minister, and the like. Established proper nouns such as the NHS are kept.
  • Every name, party, tally, date and quotation preserved exactly.

Testing

The pure derivations get vitest cases beside the existing src/lib/this-week tests:

  • The pills-or-rows threshold at its boundary (six versus seven).
  • Closest-margin-first ordering.
  • The bracket-safe trim on a summary whose third sentence opens a parenthesis it does not close.
  • Department and division shortening on the known long names.
  • A lint test that fails if any overview prose in content/this-week contains an em-dash or a connector colon.

The visual layout is checked with a headless-Chrome screenshot pass, one image per week read back and inspected, as during this design work. That discipline — render, screenshot, look, before shipping — is part of the definition of done here.

Success criteria

  • Every published week renders a one-page overview at its main URL that fits one A4 and prints as a single sheet.
  • The divisions section is correct and legible from zero divisions up to twelve and beyond, verified by screenshot.
  • The overview fits one A4 on the busiest week — twelve divisions with the brief, the top questions and the feature all present — using the defined trim order, verified by screenshot.
  • Weeks with long-form content show a working "Read the full article" link; weeks without do not.
  • The overview prose contains no em-dashes and no connector colons, enforced by the lint test.
  • A new week generated by the pipeline produces its overview block automatically, with no hand-running.
  • The six existing weeks are backfilled and unchanged on their /full pages.
