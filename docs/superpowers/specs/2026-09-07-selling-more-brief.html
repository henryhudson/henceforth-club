The funnel · what the reports say

Deck of Cards is the only funnel with a shape. Apple shows it about six and a half thousand times a week, mostly to searchers in Thailand, Vietnam, the United States and Mexico; 136 opened the product page last full week and 51 installed, between 44 and 61 every week since June: three quarters from search, a tenth to a quarter from an app referrer (the Game Center invite path), two or three from the site. Impressions doubled in late July; page views did not move and have sat between 110 and 180 a week since March. One page visitor in five installs.

Since March, 66 people started the free trial, 12 paid, 54 lapsed; every trial was on the 99p monthly, because the yearly carries none. The monthly has renewed eleven times in six months and seven converts have cancelled: two new payers a month, lost at about forty per cent a month, is exactly the base of eight.

Henceforth has a price and no funnel: 220 to 270 impressions a week, five to fifteen page views, fourteen downloads all year, one since June. The Hansard is found by browsing, almost wholly in Great Britain, and has sold ten copies since 2 July.

Deck of Cards · free, with a subscription

Charges. Free. The Multiplayer subscription is 99p a month (70p paid out in Britain, 84 cents in the United States) with a one-week trial, or £9.99 a year (£7.06 paid out) with no trial; neither is family-shareable. The buyer gets one live table over Game Center for up to thirteen players, any game; by the code read this morning an invited guest joins without paying, since the accept path has no subscription check.

Honest read. The shape is right and unsaid: nothing tells a host that friends join free. The price is below a game night's value and below the flinch; the trial leads only to the cheapest plan, which churns; seven days is shorter than the gap between two game nights.

Henceforth · £9.99 once, plus fees on chain

Charges. £9.99 up front, universal: iPhone, iPad and Mac from one sale, about £7.06 paid out; no subscription, no in-app purchase. On chain, xfolklore charges cost: an archive read is X's own charge plus a quarter, about £1.02 today, of which the site keeps about 19 pence; the inscription reward is twice the miner fee, capped, under a penny at today's $16.94 a coin.

Honest read. The only price in the portfolio worth having: one sale is ten Deck subscriber-months, and a one-off suits a maker who wants to own a tool. The fees are cost recovery, a reason to download, not income. The problem is wholly reach.

The Hansard · 99p once

Charges. 99p up front, 70p paid out; the board's note that it is free is wrong, the storefront and the sales report both show a paid app, ten sales since 2 July. No in-app purchase; the chamber, the division replay, notifications and the This Week digest are inside the price, and the digest is free on the site.

Honest read. 99p is a tip jar, correctly sized for 231 page views in two months. The watcher who pays real money, a researcher or a lobbyist, pays the trade services hundreds a year and cannot be found or priced from a base of ten. The digest is the shareable thing and is rightly free: distribution, not income.

The listings, judged from outside

Deck. First line "A complete deck of cards in your pocket. Play any card game, anywhere": true and single-player; the paid thing is the ninth line and never says friends join free. Keywords: the long list of games, right for search. First picture, three phones on one table, right; the second, "Solitaire, your way", sells the free thing.

Henceforth. "Bitcoin you can script", then a dense, honest paragraph; keywords bitcoin, wallet, forth, terminal. A real send on screen, then "Embrace the stack": right for the maker already looking, not for a Mac browser passing.

The Hansard. First line names the official record and the reader's judgement: good. The 1.11 exports put the Latest tab first, a dated list of bills, and the party map second: right for a browse-found app, since the first picture now changes daily.

The path to a few thousand a month

Three in the price, ten in the funnel, the rest in reach

Two thousand pounds a month from Deck at the new prices is about 1,300 paying subscribers. Holding 1,300 at ten per cent monthly churn means 130 new payers a month, thirty a week: at a quarter of trials converting, 120 trial starts a week; at one download in ten starting a trial, 1,200 downloads a week, twenty-five times today; at one in five and three in ten, 500, ten times.

So the multiples fix the order. First the price, a day's work that multiplies everything after it by three. Second the funnel, four steps that can each roughly double: page views per impression back to the spring's 3.3 per cent; trial starts to one in ten by saying friends join free; trial to paid to thirty per cent with a longer trial; churn, by the yearly becoming the plan the trial leads to. About eight times together, five pounds a month to forty, and every later download worth eight times as much.

Third is reach, whose size is unknown; nothing compounds yet. At five per cent a week, held, ten times takes forty-seven weeks; at ten per cent, twenty-four. The first five hundred pounds a month is about 330 payers, 320 downloads a week: eight months at five per cent, five at ten. Arithmetic, not a forecast.

Henceforth is the other route: nine sales a day from a listing seen thirty times a day is reach with a spike answer, a Show Hacker News post that fronts; twenty to a hundred sales that week, £140 to £700, once, is an estimate. The Hansard funds nothing at 99p. So: price this week, the funnel over four Wednesdays, Hacker News the week after; October's verdict reads whether downloads moved.

Killed, and why

A Hansard price rise or paid tier. Ten sales in two months and 231 page views is no base to price against; 99p to £2.99 on one sale a week is £1.40 a week. Revisit at five hundred users.

The archive fees as income. Nineteen pence of margin on a read and under a penny on the inscription, by design: xfolklore recovers X's cost and no more. It is a reason to buy the app, not a line in the books.

Pricing by storefront. Apple's equalised tiers already put Thailand at 29 baht; thirteen Thai trials produced one payer and thirteen cancellations. Nothing suggests a local price is the variable.

Localising Henceforth or The Hansard. Their buyers read English by the nature of the products (the United States, Britain and Germany; Great Britain alone).

A separate family or table product for Deck. Guests already play free; family sharing on the yearly covers a household. A new product would be a third door to the same room.

Paid acquisition stays killed by the standing zero-spend rule; nothing here needs it.
