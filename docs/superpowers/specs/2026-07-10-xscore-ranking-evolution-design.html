Design spec · 2026-07-10 · henceforth.club/text ranking · extends xScore (henceforth-club)

Every satoshi is a vote

The site already ranks posts by the money paid to them. Make the creator’s own inscription cost the first vote, and a post’s rank becomes something simple and honest: the sum of every satoshi ever committed to it — inside a window the reader can turn.

What exists. src/lib/xScore.ts is a pure, replayable fold over an append-only ledger (src/lib/xVotes.ts, x:ledger:<handle>): score(post) = Σ votes (±sats) × 2^(−daysAgo / 30). A paid vote — the funding transaction is the vote — carries direction and satoshis; its ranking influence halves every thirty days, though the satoshis themselves never decay (“money paid is money kept”). Nothing in the module touches Redis or the clock: ledger and as-of day in, score table out. That purity is the whole reason this evolution stays small.

The gap. The ranking counts what viewers spend and ignores what the creator spent to put the post on chain at all. It also has one time treatment — a fuzzy thirty-day half-life — that a reader can neither see nor steer. This spec closes both gaps without adding a second concept.

1 · The idea: cost is the founding vote

Inscribing a post costs satoshis, and the cost is credible because it is spent — a creator does not pay to put junk permanently on Bitcoin. That is costly signalling: the payment is the creator’s own stake in the post’s worth. So the upload cost is not a number to subtract from earnings or divide into them; it is earnings of the same kind — the creator up-voting their own post with the price they paid.

Cost as a founding vote, uncapped (Henry, 2026-07-10). The creator’s inscription cost enters the ledger as one up-vote, at its full value, dated the inscription day. A 14,000,000-satoshi video legitimately starts near the top because its creator committed a great deal to preserve it; viewers push it up or down from there. Rejected: earnings ÷ cost (efficiency), which would punish expensive media; earnings − cost (profit), which makes cost a subtraction; and capping the founding vote, which would blunt exactly the signal we want to reward. The one visible consequence — upload cost scales with byte size, so larger media starts higher on stake alone — is intended, not a side effect.

Why this is the load-bearing move. Once the creator’s payment and the viewers’ payments are the same type of ledger entry, there is no “cost model” and “earnings model” to reconcile. There is one ledger of committed satoshis and one pure fold, and every feature below — windows, backfill, corrections — is a replay of that fold with different bounds. The system stays small because cost never becomes a second idea.

2 · The model

For a chosen time window, a post’s score is the signed sum of every satoshi committed to it inside that window:

score(post, window) = Σ (dir === "up" ? +sats : −sats)
                      over ledger entries whose day ≥ windowStart
  • Founding vote — { txid: inscriptionTxid, postId, dir: "up", sats: uploadCost, day: inscriptionDay }. Uncapped.
  • Viewer up-votes (+) and down-votes (−) — exactly as today.

This is not literal chess Elo — there are no pairwise matches. “Elo” was shorthand for a live, dynamic score, and the committed-satoshi sum is that score. The money never decays; the window is the time control.

Windows replace the decay; default is top this week (Henry, 2026-07-10). Five windows — day, week, month, year, all — each a pure replay of the ledger over a start bound. Inside a window every entry counts at full weight; the thirty-day continuous half-life is removed, because the window is a freshness mechanism the reader can see and steer, and two overlapping time treatments are one too many. all is the undecayed lifetime sum. The feed defaults to week — right while activity is sparse, since a day window would usually be empty. It is a single exported constant; when volume grows enough that a day of committed satoshis is a lively list, the default shortens to day (or finer) with a one-line change — the five windows already exist, so it is config, never rework.
Global only — no personalization (Henry, 2026-07-10). The score is one global table, the same for every viewer. The tempting fourth factor — demote posts “a viewer has seen before” — is deliberately deleted, not deferred: tracking who-saw-what needs identity the site does not have (most visitors are anonymous; a Bitcoin address would cover a sliver and leak privacy to read), and storing it anywhere is exactly the weight worth avoiding. Freshness instead comes free from the window — open “this week” on a return visit and it has rotated with the new week’s votes, without the site remembering anything about you. The identity problem is not solved; it is removed.

3 · Architecture: extends three units, adds no subsystem

Why the fold stays the seam. Because foldScores is a pure function of (ledger, as-of day, window), a window is just another argument and the founding vote is just another entry — so the entire feature is expressible as replays, each independently testable, with no cached number ever hand-edited. A correction (a double-spent funding transaction, a re-tuned window) is a replay, exactly as it is today.

4 · Data flow, in five acts

ARCHIVE — a post lands on chain (its inscription transaction id, cost, and day are known).
FOUND — one ledger entry is appended: the creator up-votes the post with the upload cost. No new scoring path runs; the existing fold will consume it.
VOTE — viewers pay to up- or down-vote; each payment appends one ledger entry, as today.
FOLD — the ledger folds once per window into five sorted sets; a fold is a pure replay, so a rebuild is exact.
READ — /text reads the selected window (week by default) and orders the feed by it.

5 · Not twice: two-level deduplication

Two independent guards stop a post being archived — or counted — twice, each keyed on a stable identifier and each failing closed.

Level one — never inscribe a post twice (archive side). Before archiving, the handle’s on-chain state is read: archivedState(handle) returns the sets of tweet ids and media post ids already inscribed, and only the delta is inscribed. The dedup key is the tweet id — unique and immutable — and the text and media sets are independent, so a text-archived post can still gain its photos later. It fails closed: an unreadable index archives nothing rather than re-inscribing everything already paid for. This guard is the archive flow’s (a separate sub-project); the ranking depends on it holding but does not own it.

The founding vote is at most one per post. Transaction-id keying stops the same payment being counted twice, but not the same post acquiring two founding votes if level one ever slipped and inscribed it in two transactions. So the founding-vote write is conditional on the post having no founding vote yet — viewer votes are deliberately many-per-post (the crowd summing), founding votes exactly one. Even a level-one slip cannot inflate a post’s stake; the ranking is robust to the archive guard failing.

6 · Backfill: it works before the archive flow exists

The founding vote needs an inscription’s cost, transaction id, and day. New archives will write it at inscription time — but that write belongs to the archive flow, which is a separate sub-project pending the user’s own X export. So this project stands alone by reconstructing founding votes for already-archived posts from the chain: an inscription transaction’s cost is its miner fee (sum of input values minus sum of output values), read once per post from WhatsOnChain, appended as the founding vote. The backfill is idempotent — the founding vote is keyed by the inscription transaction id, so a replay never double-counts.

7 · Failure and edges

CaseBehaviour
Unreadable ledger dayContributes nothing to the table, rather than turning it to NaN — existing behaviour, preserved.
A post net-negative (down-votes exceed up-votes and the founding vote)Allowed to go negative. A post the crowd paid to bury below the creator’s own stake is honest signal; clamping to zero would hide it. It simply sorts to the bottom.
Chosen window is empty (no committed satoshis inside it)The feed falls back to reverse-chronological, so an empty “today” is never a blank page.
Backfill can’t read an inscription’s fee (chain read fails)The post keeps its viewer-vote score with no founding vote yet; the backfill retries next run. Fail-open: a missing founding vote understates a rank, it never corrupts one.

8 · Testing

xScore stays a pure fold, so it is exercised the way xScore.test.ts already is — example and property tests over (ledger, as-of day, window), no mocks, no clock:

  • A founding vote raises its post’s score by exactly the upload cost inside every window that contains its day.
  • An entry dated exactly on windowStart is included; one day earlier is excluded (boundary).
  • Removing the continuous decay: a vote’s weight inside a window is its full ±sats, independent of age.
  • Down-votes can drive a post negative, and the ordering respects it.
  • Backfill idempotence: appending the same founding vote (same inscription transaction id) twice yields one contribution.
  • Founding vote at-most-one-per-post: a second founding vote for a post that already has one — even under a different inscription transaction id — is rejected, so a level-one dedup slip cannot double-count the creator’s stake.

9 · Scope and sequencing

In scope: the founding-vote entry, the windowed fold, the five cached sorted sets, the backfill, and the /text window toggle defaulting to week.

Out of scope, by decision: per-viewer personalization (deleted, §2); and the X-export archive flow that produces new inscription costs — a separate sub-project blocked on the user’s X data export, which this design integrates with later by writing the founding vote at inscription time instead of by backfill.

Merge discipline. This touches the vote ledger, which is money-path code, so it gets an adversarial review before merge (this repo’s standing rule). And a push to main here is a production deploy — so the backfill runs against the live ledger only after that review, and the founding-vote write is idempotent by inscription transaction id precisely so a re-run during rollout cannot double-count.
