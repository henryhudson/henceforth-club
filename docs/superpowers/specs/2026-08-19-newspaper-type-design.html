The Newspaper Measure — small type, real columns, one sheet

Both print editions already wear a newspaper's clothes. This spec gives them a newspaper's body: type at the size the trade actually prints, columns at the width it actually cuts, and a page you lean into — on paper, with a magnifying glass if you like.

The ask (2026-08-19): make the newspaper reports — The Hansard weekly digest and the daily Morning Edition — more newspaper-like: smaller type, denser, zoomed-into rather than read at arm's length, and still readable on paper with a magnifying glass. What follows is the research, the proposed measure, a finished true-scale prototype of both sheets built from entirely real copy, and the implementation plan. Nothing in the live renderers has been touched.

The trade's constants, across every masthead: body text between 8 and 10 point on leading only 105 to 115 percent of the size, justified with hyphenation in columns too narrow to lose your place in, first-line indents instead of paragraph spacing, a hard hierarchy of rules (heavy cut-off rules over section titles, hairlines between columns), and a separate, smaller universe — agate, 5.5pt — for everything tabular: scores, listings, markets, classifieds.

The decisive physics. Newspapers stop at 8pt not for the reader's eyes but for the press: newsprint drinks ink, and the Financial Times budgets for 30 percent dot gain. A home laser at 600 dots to the inch has essentially none, so an A4 report can print below newsprint sizes and stay sharper than the Financial Times does. Six point is the trade consensus floor for legibility on a 600 dot printer; agate at 5.5pt is exactly what a 2x to 3x magnifying glass is for — and the Guinness-record smallest newspaper (Terra Nostra, 2012) is sold to be read with a 6x to 8x glass. The magnifier is not a compromise here; it is the reading mode the brief asked for.

Key sources: Fonts In Use on the Times Modern redesign (fontsinuse.com/uses/3368), the Guardian 2005 house-style notes, the Financial Times commercial print specification (marketing.nikkei.com), Wikipedia on agate and on Retina, Fast Company on Retina's eight-page saving, Eye Magazine "Forensic types", Butterick's Practical Typography on point size, Guinness World Records "smallest newspaper". The oft-quoted "New York Times body is 8.7pt" could not be verified and appears to be a conflation with the London Times — not relied on.

Both are set like a book, 40 to 50 percent larger than a paper. And the Hansard sheet has a live quirk worth naming: A4Sheet.tsx auto-fits by stepping the root font size between 6.6pt and 14pt, but nearly every other size in overview.module.css is pinned in points — so a light recess week balloons the body toward 14pt while the section titles stay frozen at 8.3pt, and the hierarchy drifts apart.

3 · The proposed measure

RoleSettingRationale
BodyGeorgia 7pt on 8pt, justified, hyphenated, first-line indents, no paragraph spacingBelow newsprint's floor, above the laser printer's; Georgia's big x-height is built for small sizes
Columns43.5mm legs, 4mm gutters, 0.5pt hairline rules — four to the sheetThe trade's 11-pica measure, landed on A4 inside the existing 12mm margins
Page cutModular, not a uniform four-column run: one big lead module across three legs with a boxed sidebar, then bands of ruled and boxed squares of varying widths (three-and-one, two-and-two), the big feature square anchoring the foot of the pageHenry's direction (19 August): "the newspapers use a lot of squares at the bottom and a big one for the main story"
Agate5.5pt on 6.6pt, the system sans, tabular figuresFor every listing: downloads, divisions, departments, ship list, decisions — the Wall Street Journal's stock-table size
HeadlinesLead about 25pt tight (1.03), italic deck at 8.6pt, section rubrics 6pt capitals under a 1.5pt cut-off ruleScaled from broadsheet convention to the A4 sheet
FurnitureFolio line between hairline rules; three-line drop caps; boxed feature; full-width colophon at the footThe page furniture that makes a paper read as a paper
NameplatesThe Morning Edition in blackletter (UnifrakturMaguntia, Georgia fallback); The Hansard in letter-spaced roman capitalsThe New York Times and Telegraph convention for the daily; The Times of London convention for the paper of record

What the measure buys. Going from 9.75pt on 1.45 in two columns to 7pt on 8pt in four is roughly a 2.5-times gain in words per sheet. Concretely: today's two-sheet daily fits on one sheet with a quarter-page to spare — one page, one inscription, halving the daily inscription cost while carrying more than today's two pages. And the Hansard's fit loop stops ballooning recess weeks to 14pt; the reclaimed space takes standing agate matter instead — divisions in full, the new 650-member register sweep, the written-answer picks.

4 · The prototype — built, rendered, verified

A finished true-scale prototype sits beside this spec: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-19-newspaper-type-prototype.html, with its print-ready render 2026-08-19-newspaper-type-prototype.pdf. Three A4 sheets, every word real, all cut modular: sheet one re-sets the Morning Edition of 19 August (No. 57, the screenshot-uploader review) from content/board/reports/2026-08-19.json; sheet two re-sets this week's Hansard digest — the 12 to 19 August recess issue, the Clacton by-election — from its live draft content/this-week/2026-08-19.json, with standing matter (the last sitting week, the bills waiting for the return) filling what recess leaves quiet; sheet three keeps the heavy 15 to 22 July week (Andy Burnham takes office) as the stress demonstration.

The magnifier test. Print the PDF at 100 percent with printer margins off (it is margin-exact A4). Naked eye: the headlines, decks and 7pt body should read at arm's length in good light. With a 2x to 3x glass: the 5.5pt agate — the downloads table, the divisions, the department listings — should resolve crisply, serifs intact. If the agate smudges, the printer is below 600 dots to the inch; the spec then keeps 7pt body but lifts agate to 6pt.

Verified this session: the render (Puppeteer against the file, the house pattern) produces exactly two A4 pages; a column-geometry probe confirms zero overflow in both axes on both sheets — nothing clipped, nothing stranded past column four. Render at any time with:

5 · Implementation, when the word is given

5.1 The Hansard digest (safe to change freely — never publishes)

  • Re-cut overview.module.css to the measure: root at 7pt, four columns, agate listings; convert every pinned point size to em so the whole hierarchy scales as one voice.
  • Re-bound the A4Sheet.tsx fit loop to the new world: start 7pt, floor 6pt, ceiling 8.5pt — dense weeks tighten a little, light weeks grow a little, and nothing balloons.
  • Verify with render-draft.mjs against three recent weeks (a heavy one, 2026-07-22; a recess one, 2026-08-19) — one page each, visual pass on the PNG.

5.2 The Morning Edition (money-adjacent — every test render through --out)

  • Re-cut the print block of globals.css (lines 608 to 705): four columns at 4mm gutters with 0.5pt rules, body to 7pt on 8pt, listings and decisions to agate, rubric and folio treatment per the prototype.
  • Return the daily budget to one page in render-pdf.mjs (BUDGET.daily = 1) — the 4 August reversal to two pages was about content not fitting; at this measure it fits with headroom, and the inscription cost halves.
  • Verify with --out renders of three recent dates before any live morning runs it; the 987px height cliff and the block-measure scripts still apply, at the new scale.
