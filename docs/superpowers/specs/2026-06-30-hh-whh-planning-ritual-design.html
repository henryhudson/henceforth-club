The planning ritual — /hh daily, /whh weekly

Design · 2026-06-30 · how the daily and weekly reviews come to co-own one living plan: /whh sets the week ahead, /hh judges yesterday and plans today, and the /board/week planner is the shared artifact between them · decision points flagged inline

One-line: /hh stops being only a code-processor and becomes a daily judgement-and-planning ritual — "was yesterday good, and was it even the right thing to focus on? then, what best earns today?" — writing today's plan into the same week planner that /whh lays out every Sunday or Monday. The plan is a living document, re-evaluated each morning, not a Monday snapshot that is stale by Wednesday.

Status: the planner surface already ships (full-width colour-coded Sunday→Saturday grid, tickable major events on top, an empty 0–24h timeline beneath). This spec covers the behavioural change — what /hh and /whh each write into it — not the UI, which is built.

The shift

Today /hh re-adjudicates the automated review emails and reconciles the Morning Board — it processes code. The change is to give it a second, higher job each morning: a verdict on yesterday and a plan for today. The daily review becomes the place where the week's direction is corrected, not just where findings are triaged. /whh keeps its forward, weekly job; /hh gains the daily steering.

/hh's morning sequence

The existing daily steps stay; a judgement-and-planning layer is appended after them.

StepWhat happensState

The evaluation — "was yesterday the right focus?"

This judgement is the heart of the change, so its anchor matters.

Proposed anchor decision 1: judge focus primarily against the week's major events — did yesterday's work move a planned milestone forward? — with a secondary, honest impact read: even if it was on the plan, did it matter, or was it motion? Pure plan-adherence alone would reward box-ticking; pure gut-feel alone drifts. The two together ask both "did you do what you said" and "was what you said worth doing." The alternative you raised — a broader, impact-only judgement untethered from the plan — is the other candidate; this is the call to confirm.

The quality half is cheap: /hh already produces it (confirmed-vs-rejected, verdicts that survived adversarial refutation, new confirmed defects). The focus half is the new reasoning — comparing the day's actual work against the planner's major events for the week — and it is what feeds step 3.

The shared artifact — the week planner

Both rituals read and write one object, already live: board:week:<weekOf> (mirrored to content/board/weeks/<date>.json), rendered at the gated /board/week. Its retro.weekPlan is the seven-day plan.

retro.weekPlan[day] = {
  date, weekday, isReviewDay,           // Wednesday ★ carries the article/review marker
  tasks: [                              // the day's MAJOR EVENTS (top row, tickable)
    "Submit Hansard v1",                //   plain label, or…
    { label, start, end }               //   …a timed block on the 0–24h grid
  ]
}

What /hh writes, concretely

Each morning /hh patches the current week's weekPlan and republishes it (the same Upstash path the daily board already uses — no deploy needed). Three writes:

  • Today's major events — re-derived for the current weekday from yesterday's verdict and the board's live state, replacing any stale entry.
  • Mark done — milestones that shipped get a done flag, rendered struck-through. decision 2: this server-side done is distinct from your in-browser checkbox ticks (local storage) — the page would show both, the plan's own "shipped" state and your personal tick. Keep them separate, or have one drive the other?
  • Adjust the days ahead — only when the week genuinely moved (a milestone slipped, a new one appeared); otherwise the future days are left as /whh set them.

Engine — same hybrid boundary as today

Reuse the trust boundary the reviews already run on: deterministic facts, agent judgement.

DeterministicWhich milestones shipped (board → Done), yesterday's review counts, which weekday "today" is — facts, computed, not narrated.
Agent synthesisThe verdict (good? right focus?) and today's plan — a small Workflow reading the deterministic facts + the week's major events as ground truth, never recomputing them.

State of each app — are we earning our users?

Beyond the retrospective and the downloads table, /whh gains a per-app panel answering the question under all of this: is each app worth its users' time? Most of it is fed by the App Analytics feed already being registered with Apple (the ongoing report requests are live; the data backfills in a day or two), so this section lights up as that lands.

DimensionWhat it answersSource
Reach — how bigActive users now, and total installs to dateApp Analytics — App Usage (active devices) + cumulative downloads
Growth — usersNew users this week vs last; are they sticking (retention)?App Analytics — downloads + retention curves
Effectiveness — is it usefulSessions per active user, retention, ratings — do people come back?App Analytics — App Usage + App Store ratings
Efficiency — is it well-builtCrash rate, hang rate, launch time, binary sizeApp Analytics — Performance reports + the build's size
Value verdictA weekly read: is this app earning its users, and the one change that would make it more useful?Agent synthesis over the rows above

Why this belongs in the weekly, not the daily. User value moves on a weekly-or-slower scale — retention, engagement, and ratings do not change morning to morning. The daily /hh judges yesterday's work; the weekly /whh judges whether the apps themselves are providing value. Same hybrid engine: the numbers come deterministically from App Analytics, the value verdict is the agent's. Until the analytics instances backfill, the panel shows downloads + ratings and the verdict stays qualitative decision 5.

Out of scope (v1)

  • Auto-logging the timeline — the 0–24h grid stays hand-filled for now; /hh may later record actuals, but not in this pass decision 3.
  • Changing the planner UI — it is built; this is a data/behaviour change only.
  • A second daily web surface — today's plan lives in the planner, not on a new page.
  • Automating the work itself — /hh plans and judges; you (and I, on the day) do the work.

Open decisions — to settle before the plan

  1. Evaluation anchor — focus judged primarily against the week's major events (proposed), or a broader impact-only read?
  2. Done-state — should /hh's server-side "shipped" mark and your personal checkbox tick be separate, or one drive the other?
  3. Timeline — confirm it stays hand-filled in v1 (no /hh auto-logging yet).
  4. Major-event altitude — is one-to-two milestones per day the right height, or higher (only the genuinely big events)?
  5. App-state metrics — are Reach / Growth / Effectiveness / Efficiency the right four dimensions, and is "active users + retention + crash rate + ratings" the concrete signal set, or do you want different signals?
