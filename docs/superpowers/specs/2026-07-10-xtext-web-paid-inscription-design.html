Design spec · 2026-07-10 · henceforth.club/x + Mac mini worker · scope: B of four (the last)

The pound that signs itself away

A visitor with no app pays a QR and their X archive appears on Bitcoin. For a few minutes, one key on one machine holds their pound — and the whole design exists to make those minutes as small, as bounded, and as honest as possible.

What exists. Specs A, C, and D shipped 2026-07-09, and every one is non-custodial by construction: the showroom reads archives from Bitcoin; the binding proves handle ownership by signature; the bounty routes rewards through addresses derived from an extended public key. The site holds no key and no coin anywhere. The design-panel verdict of 2026-07-02 said it plainly: money never runs through site-held funds.

The gap. A visitor lands on henceforth.club/x, sees the showroom, wants their own profile inscribed — and owns no iPhone, or won’t buy the app for a one-off. Today their only path is the App Store. Spec B gives them a web path: upload your own X export, pay a QR, and a worker inscribes it for you. Someone must sign that inscription, and the visitor has no key. So for the first time, briefly, we do.

The custody decision (Henry, 2026-07-10): ephemeral per-job custody. Not a hot wallet — a hot job. Each job gets a fresh random keypair; the visitor’s payment lands on that job’s address and becomes the sole funding input of the inscription transaction itself; the premium leaves for the cold revenue address in the same transaction; the key material is deleted the moment the job completes. The system never has a balance — only an unspent output with a countdown. Alternatives rejected: a funded hot wallet on the mini (exactly the pooled-funds surface the standing verdict forbids), and a fully non-custodial payment-request framing where the visitor’s own wallet signs the inscription (purest, but it depends on third-party wallets accepting arbitrary output scripts, which is patchy — a plain address QR is the one thing every wallet on earth can pay).

1 · Product shape

Who. A visitor on henceforth.club/x without the app. They bring their own X data export — the zip X gives every account holder — which is the policy-clean source: no X API read, no third-party fetch, the user redistributing their own data at their own direction.

What they get. Their posts inscribed on Bitcoin in exactly the archive shape the app produces, byte-compatible with the showroom reader, rendering at /x/<handle> like every other archive.

What it costs. More than the app — deliberately. The premium is the nudge toward buying Henceforth, where archiving is cheaper and non-custodial.

Pricing (Henry, 2026-07-10): fee plus a fixed satoshi premium. price = estimatedInscriptionFee(bytes) + WEB_PREMIUM_SATS. The fee half reads the same archive-cost-fixture.json that already pins the app estimator and the site estimator to identical numbers — three readers, one fixture. The premium is one exported constant, roughly a pound at the day’s rate, re-tuned by hand when the rate drifts. Rejected: a pound-pegged premium (puts a live price feed inside the money path) and a flat all-in price (a big archive could cost more in miner fees than it collects).
Reward (Henry, 2026-07-10): none on the web path. The web visitor archives their own profile and pays for the privilege; routing a bounty back to them is circular and eats the premium. The 50,000-satoshi archive reward stays an app-side incentive. The web price is miner fee + premium, nothing else, and spec D’s routing is untouched.

Scope, version one: text only. The media toggles render greyed with “get the app.” This keeps the upload within the job pipeline’s size budget, keeps version one’s money path small enough to review adversarially, and sharpens the buy-the-app nudge the premium already makes. Media follows once the plumbing has survived real users. Version one archive budget: parsed archive JSON of at most 1,000,000 bytes; larger exports are told, at upload time and before any payment, to get the app.

2 · The pipeline, in five acts

QUOTE — on the site, no money yet.
The visitor uploads their export zip. The site parses it server-side into the archive shape (text posts only), pins the result with a content hash, prices it against the shared fixture, and writes a job record: { jobId, handle, contentHash, priceSats, state: "quoted", expiresAt }. The page shows the price, the permanence warning (“Bitcoin is forever — this cannot be deleted”), and the claimed-handle notice where it applies (§5). The visitor confirms both.
KEY — on the mini, before any payment.
The worker polls for quoted jobs, generates a fresh random keypair for each, persists it encrypted under a Keychain-held wrapping key, and publishes the job’s address back to the record. Only now does the page render the QR: a plain bitcoin: address + exact amount. Key material never exists anywhere but the mini; Vercel and Upstash carry only the address.
FUND — the visitor pays.
The worker watches the job address. A payment at or above the quote moves the job to funded. Below the quote, the worker waits — top-ups are allowed until expiry — then sweeps back whatever arrived. Payment identity is the address itself: there is no user-supplied transaction id to replay, which is strictly stronger than the current payment=<txid> parameter on the archive endpoint.
INSCRIBE — one transaction, custody in and out.
The worker builds and signs a single transaction: input = the job UTXO (the visitor’s own payment); outputs = the archive inscription, byte-identical in shape to an app archive, plus the premium to the hardcoded cold revenue address. No change returns to the job address; nothing is retained. Broadcast rides the existing ARC path with its failover. The job records the transaction id and moves to inscribed.
REGISTER & DONE — back on the site.
The job registers the archive through the existing /api/x/register gate (§5) — which also stamps the handle into the /text directory (§6) — the page polls the job to done, and the archive renders at /x/<handle>. The worker deletes the job’s key material. Custody is over; its entire lifetime was the gap between FUND and INSCRIBE.

3 · Architecture: who holds what

ConcernVercel (site)Mac mini (worker)
Export upload + parse + hash + quote✓—
Job records + status polling✓ (Upstash: x:job:<id>; archive payload alongside, deleted on completion)reads + advances state
Key generation, storage, signingnever✓ — per-job random key, at rest encrypted under a Keychain wrapping key, deleted on completion
Payment watching, broadcast, sweep—✓ (WhatsOnChain watch, ARC broadcast with failover)
Registration + index✓ (existing gate, unchanged)calls it

Why the split is load-bearing. Keys are generated where they are used and nowhere else; a compromise of the site or its environment cannot steal funds, because there are none to steal there. Keys are deliberately not derived from any extended private key — one compromised job key is one job’s pound, and no master secret for this system exists on any machine. The premium forwards to cold storage inside the inscription transaction itself, so even the mini accumulates nothing.

The job state machine (persisted, every transition idempotent, resumable after a worker crash):

quoted ──key published──▶ awaiting-payment ──amount ≥ quote──▶ funded
                              │ expiry (any sats present → sweeping)
                              ▼
funded ──build+broadcast ok──▶ inscribed ──register ok──▶ done (key deleted)
   │ broadcast rejected after N retries
   ▼
sweeping ──refund lands──▶ swept (key deleted; job failed, reason recorded)

4 · Failure, refunds, and the sweep

Refunds (Henry, 2026-07-10): automatic sweep back to the payer. After bounded retries or at quote expiry, the job key signs one last transaction returning the job UTXO to the first input address of the funding payment, minus the sweep’s own miner fee. That address convention is the standard wallet-refund heuristic — right for ordinary wallets, imperfect for exotic payers, and the upload page says so in one line. Rejected: burning failed payments (a visitor who paid for a size-rejected archive eats a pound — support pain), and manual refunds (keys retained indefinitely, the exact surface ephemeral custody exists to avoid).
FailureBehaviour
Underpaid at expirySweep whatever arrived back to the payer; job swept.
Paid after expirySame sweep — the watcher keeps watching an expired job’s address for LATE_WATCH_DAYS, refunding stragglers.
Double payment / overpayFirst sufficient payment funds the job; excess and duplicates sweep back after completion.
Broadcast rejected (ARC, both providers, after BROADCAST_RETRIES)Sweep back; job swept with the rejection reason recorded for the morning review.
Worker crash mid-jobJob state + encrypted key survive on disk; the worker resumes the state machine on restart. Broadcast is idempotent by transaction id.
The sweep itself failsRetried on a capped backoff until it lands — the one case a key outlives its job, bounded by sweep success. A job stuck in sweeping past a day surfaces on the Morning Board as an ops card.
Vercel or Upstash compromiseCannot steal funds or keys (none present). Residual risk: a tampered job record could show an attacker’s address in the QR before payment. Bounded to one job’s payment; the worker publishes addresses only for jobs it created keys for, and the page renders only worker-published addresses.
Mini compromiseIn-flight job keys (at most MAX_CONCURRENT_JOBS × one quote each) plus future jobs until rotated. No float, no history of funds, premium already forwarded cold every job.

5 · Registration meets the binding (spec C)

The register gate shipped in spec C stands unchanged: an unclaimed handle registers freely; a claimed handle registers only with the owner key’s signature. The web visitor has no key, so:

  • Unclaimed handle (the common case for a no-app visitor): the job registers normally and the archive joins the index.
  • Claimed handle: the inscription still lands on Bitcoin — the chain is permissionless — but the site index will not list it without the owner’s signature. The quote page detects this before payment and says exactly what the visitor gets: “this handle’s archive index is claimed; your inscription will exist on chain, and the owner can register it from the app.” No surprise after money moves.

6 · The directory — henceforth.club/text

Everyone who uploaded, in one place. /text is the public directory of every archived profile: one row per handle, newest activity first — handle, post count, latest inscription date, the verified tick where the handle is claimed (spec C), each row linking to the archive at /x/<handle>. It is also the web path’s front door: the “archive yours” call to action at the top starts the upload flow of §2.

The missing primitive is one key. Today the index is per-handle only (x:<handle> → ordered txids); nothing can answer “who has uploaded?”. The directory adds a single global sorted set, x:handles (member = handle, score = latest registration time), written in the same breath as appendXTxid / setXTxids by the existing registration path — so the directory inherits the register gate exactly: what appears is what registered. A claimed handle’s unregistered web inscription (§5) exists on chain but does not appear until the owner registers it, which is the same honesty the archive pages already have. One-time backfill: scan the existing x:* keys. Null-Redis safe like every sibling module; rebuildable by scanning, like the index it extends.

7 · Policy and permanence

  • Source: only the visitor’s own X export, uploaded by them — no X API fetch anywhere in this path, no quota cost, no third-party redistribution. The upload page carries an “this is my own account’s export” assertion.
  • Permanence: inscription is irreversible and the visitor confirms a plain-words warning before the QR renders. The inscribing act is theirs, at their direction; we are the pen, not the author.
  • Retention: the parsed archive payload lives in the job record only until done or swept, then is deleted — the durable copy is the one the visitor paid to put on Bitcoin.

9 · Testing & the review gate

  • Pure core, test-first, all deterministic with stubbed keys and a stubbed clock: the quote fold (pinned to archive-cost-fixture.json); the export parser (real export fixtures, including an oversize one); the job state machine (every transition in §3–4, every sweep branch, crash-resume); the payment matcher (exact, under, over, late, duplicate); construction of both transactions (inscription shape byte-compared against an app-produced archive fixture; sweep fee arithmetic); the directory index (registration stamps x:handles, the claimed-unregistered case stays absent, backfill is idempotent).
  • Adversarial review before merge — this is a money-and-key path; it gets the full treatment the wallet gets.
  • Manual end-to-end with the low-funds wallet before the QR renders for anyone else: pay a real quote, watch the inscription land, watch a deliberately-underpaid job sweep back. Real keys and real money stay out of automated tests, per the standing rule.
  • Ops: stuck jobs (sweeping > a day, awaiting-payment backlog) surface as Morning Board ops cards via the daily routine.

Approved in brainstorm 2026-07-10 (custody, pricing, refunds, reward — Henry). Implementation plan follows on spec sign-off; build order puts the pure core first and the QR last, so money can only arrive once everything behind it is green.
